Homes in Mission Viejo

Mission Viejo is one of the largest master-planned communities ever built in the country under a single project. The first homes sold in 1966 and the city kept filling in through the 1990s. That long build is the thing that matters when you are quoting a roof.

The early phases are low, wide, single-story ranch homes. Shallow roof pitch, deep eaves, a simple gable or hip, and a lot of visible frontage for the size of the house. Those take one clean run of C9s and they look great, because the whole roof reads from the street.

The 1980s and 1990s tracts are a different shape. Two-story stucco elevations, steeper pitches, concrete tile roofs, and a front that stacks vertically instead of spreading sideways. On those homes the roofline outlines the upper story, so the light sits higher and carries further down the street.

Both shapes are normal work. What changes is the amount of line, the working height, and the clip. Tile needs a different clip than composition shingle. Neither one needs a nail or a staple, because we do not put fasteners into anybody's roof. Two homes on the same street here can be twenty-five years apart in age, so we would rather look at your actual frontage than guess from the address.

Communities and HOAs in Mission Viejo

A lot of the city sits under an association, and the associations are not all alike. Casta del Sol and Palmia are large 55+ communities. Canyon Crest is guard-gated. The neighborhoods around Lake Mission Viejo often carry lake membership on top of the usual dues, and older pockets like Madrid have their own rules again.

Many communities around here publish lighting guidelines — dates the display can go up and has to come down, which colors are fine, whether a timer is required. We are not going to claim we know your association's rulebook, because we have not read it. Tell us the community and what the guidelines say and we design inside them. If something you want is not allowed, you hear that before you pay anything.

What a display costs in Mission Viejo

Two packages, two published bands, the same in every city we cover: $400–$800 for the Basic Roofline and $1,000–$2,500 for the Premium Full Front.

What moves a Mission Viejo home inside a band: the feet of roofline you want lit, whether we are working at one story or two, how cut-up the roof is, and how much of the rest of the property joins in — entry, garage, trees, bushes, pathway.

A wide single-story ranch home and a narrow two-story tract home can land in the same band for completely different reasons. That is exactly why we quote by package instead of by the foot. The drive never moves the price; the house does.

Questions we get from Mission Viejo

My roof is concrete tile. Does that cost more?

No, tile does not carry a surcharge. It changes which clip we bring, not the price band and not the fastener — nothing gets nailed or stapled into a tile roof. Concrete tile is common on the 1980s and 1990s tracts here, so just mention it when you send the photos and we turn up with the right clips.

I live in a 55+ community like Casta del Sol or Palmia. Can you work there?

Yes. The homes are usually single-story with a short, simple roofline, which often means a tidy Basic Roofline job rather than a full front. Two things are worth checking first: what your community's guidelines say about seasonal lighting, and whether visitor or contractor access needs arranging. Tell us both when you book and the install day runs without a hitch.

I am behind a guard gate. How does install day work?

Give us the community name and the gate procedure when you book, and leave word with the gate for the date we agree. It takes one message and saves standing at the kerb on the morning. Canyon Crest is the best known guard-gated community here, but several smaller ones work the same way.
