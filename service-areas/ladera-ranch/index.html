Homes in Ladera Ranch

Ladera Ranch is unincorporated and was built from about 2000 onward as nine planned villages: Avendale, Bridgepark, Covenant Hills, Echo Ridge, Flintridge, Oak Knoll, Terramor, Township and Wycliffe. Because it all went up inside one short window, the housing is consistent in age and the streets have a strong, deliberate look.

Oak Knoll is the original village, with Craftsman-style homes. Avendale is the largest, anchored by Founders Park. Covenant Hills is the only gated village and the one where custom and semi-custom homes were built. Bridgepark and Flintridge sit closest to the commercial spine and hold more of the condos and townhomes.

The detail that matters for lighting is the front porch. A lot of Ladera Ranch homes were designed with a proper covered porch and the front elevation set close to the street. That gives you two lines to work with instead of one — the main roofline above and the porch roof below — and outlining both is what makes these houses look so good lit. Add the fact that many streets have the garage off a rear lane, and the front of the house is almost entirely porch, columns, door and roof. That is a full-front kind of elevation even when the house is not large.

Communities and HOAs in Ladera Ranch

There are two layers of rules here, not one, and knowing that in advance saves everybody time.

LARMAC, the Ladera Ranch Maintenance Corporation, is the master association and handles architectural approvals across the whole community. Underneath it sit seventeen sub-associations, mostly in the condo and townhome neighborhoods, each with its own documents.

The good news is that LARMAC's published Aesthetic Standards answer the holiday question directly. Holiday lighting and decorations may be displayed without ARC approval from sixty days before a holiday to thirty days after it, up to ninety days total in any twelve-month period, and the display should relate to the holiday being celebrated. Permanent exterior changes still need ARC approval before installation, which is one reason a clip-on seasonal display is the easy route here. If you are in a condo or townhome, your sub-association may have its own rules on top — check those, send us what they say, and the design is built inside them. If something you want is not allowed, you hear it before you pay anything.

What a display costs in Ladera Ranch

Two packages, two published bands, the same in every city we cover: $400–$800 for the Basic Roofline and $1,000–$2,500 for the Premium Full Front.

What moves a Ladera Ranch home inside a band: how many feet of roofline you want lit, whether the porch roof is included as a second line, the working height, and whether the entry, columns, trees, bushes and pathway join in.

Because the lots are close-set and the fronts are near the street, a display here reads at close range. That is an argument for doing fewer things well rather than lighting everything — a clean double line on the roof and porch usually beats a scattered full front on these elevations, and it costs less. We will tell you when that is the better call.

How booking works in Ladera Ranch

  1. Count your days. LARMAC allows holiday lighting without approval from sixty days before a holiday to thirty days after, ninety days total a year; if you are in a sub-association, check its rules too.

Questions we get from Ladera Ranch

Do I ask LARMAC or my own village association?

For a seasonal display, usually neither: LARMAC's Aesthetic Standards allow holiday lighting without ARC approval inside the sixty-days-before, thirty-days-after window. If you are in one of the seventeen sub-associations — most common in the condo and townhome neighborhoods — that one has its own documents on top, so check those. Send us anything they say and we design to it.

Does a temporary Christmas display really need approval?

Not from LARMAC, as long as you stay inside the window: their published Aesthetic Standards say holiday lighting and decorations may be displayed without ARC approval from sixty days before a holiday to thirty days after, and no more than ninety days in any twelve-month period. An install in late October or November with a January takedown fits that comfortably. Permanent fixtures are a different story and do need approval, which is why everything we hang is clip-on and comes down.

My garage is off the back lane, so the front is just a porch. What gets lit?

Quite a lot, actually — those are some of the best-looking fronts in the area. The main roofline and the porch roof give you two parallel lines of C9s, and the columns, the door surround, a wrapped tree and the pathway fill in the rest. It is a full-front shape on a compact house, and it reads very well because everything sits close to the street.
