Icicles, small palms and composition roofs

Three practical questions that come out of these requests and the houses here. We wrote them; they are not anyone’s words.

Can icicles go on instead of C9 bulbs?

Yes, along the roof edge in warm white or pure white; multicolor icicles are a special order. They are measured by the length of the roofline like bulbs are, but our published per-foot rate is for C9s, so the icicle figure is the one written on your quote. More on icicles.

How are a few small palms priced?

One at a time, each from its own photo, with the whole tree in frame. A short palm by the walk is a smaller job than one that clears the roof. The lights wrap the trunk; nothing is drilled in. Palm trees, explained.

Does a shingle roof take the same clip as tile?

No. Composition shingle has an edge a clip can grab, and tile has none, so each gets its own hardware. Either way nothing is nailed or stapled. Roof-safe clips.

The Colony downtown and the hills to the east

Anaheim has two very different housing ends. The Anaheim Colony Historic District, the flat grid around downtown, covers about 1.8 square miles and holds over 1,000 qualifying historic buildings; the city adopted it in 1997. Most homes there are one-story Craftsman bungalows and Spanish-eclectic cottages from the 1920s on small, narrow lots, roofed in composition shingle at a low pitch or a gable, not tile.

A one-story bungalow keeps the whole eave within ladder reach, and the clips hook onto the shingle edge. A narrow lot tends to mean a short front, and fewer feet to price.

Anaheim Hills was largely built out from the 1970s to the 1990s as hillside tracts and custom estates: two-story stucco houses under Mediterranean tile, on sloping lots that face the view. That is a second story to reach and tile to clip, on ground that is not level.

Associations are common in the hills. Canyon Heights and Anaheim Hills Estates are named homeowners’ associations, and Canyon Heights sits under the larger Anaheim Hills Planned Community Association. Down in the Colony, most lots stand on their own with no association. If you have one, name it on the form. HOA rules and lights.
