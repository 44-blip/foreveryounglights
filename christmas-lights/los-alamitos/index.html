“Red and white with the porch,” October 2026

The request came by text, with a photo of the house to follow. One line of it said what to light: “Red and white with the porch.” We checked the roofline from above, then sent back a quote with both looks pictured.

Two choices sit inside that line. One is the color mix. The other is what “the porch” means on a given house: the porch roof edge, the posts or columns holding it up, or both. A quote spells out each piece as its own line, so it is easy to see what the porch adds.

Red and white, porch columns and what to send

Written by us from that request and the houses in town. Nobody asked these in so many words.

Can red and white alternate along the whole roofline?

Yes. Our color guide lists red and white among its C9 mixes, set bulb by bulb along the edge. Ask before booking if you want something fancier than a plain alternation, since a custom pattern may cost more. Colors and mixes.

How do porch columns get lit without glue?

With tension. Straps cinch the lights at the column’s top and base so they hug it, and nothing is stuck on. You can have each column fully wrapped or just outlined up its edges. Columns are priced one by one, not by the foot. Pillars and columns.

Carrier Row and the postwar grid

Los Alamitos is mostly postwar housing on a flat street grid. On the east side, Carrier Row went up as three separate infill subdivisions between 1947 and 1955, and its streets carry the names of aircraft carriers from the Second World War. Old Town West and College Park North are mainly single-story homes from the 1940s to the 1960s. Rossmoor Highlands is another named neighborhood inside the city.

Most roofs are low-pitched and covered in composition shingle, as postwar tracts usually were, and the lots are modest infill sizes rather than large parcels.

For a quote, those are friendly facts. A single-story edge at a gentle slope stays within easy reach, and shingles give a clip something to hold, so no fastener goes into the roof.

We found no mandatory homeowners’ association anywhere inside city limits; the houses are older than the era when HOAs became common. Nearby Rossmoor and College Park East have their own bodies, but both sit outside Los Alamitos.
