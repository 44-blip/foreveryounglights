Villa Park · service area

Christmas Light Installation in Villa Park

Villa Park is an Orange County city of big lots. Every lot must be at least 20,000 square feet, about half an acre, and much of the housing is single-story ranch homes. Forever Young Lighting has no Villa Park home lit yet, so this page sticks to what houses like these mean for a display.

Roofline $400–$800 · Full front $1,000–$2,500

Half-acre zoning and 1960s ranch tracts

The city’s zoning sets that 20,000-square-foot minimum everywhere. A large part of the town was built as tracts in the early to middle 1960s, and those homes are mostly one story.

Several custom-lot subdivisions go bigger still, from half an acre to two acres, with the long street frontage you see on horse property. City code allows horses on any lot larger than 10,000 square feet.

Named communities include Cerro Villa Heights, Gainsborough Homes, Villa Park Orchards, Somerset and Havenwood Farms.

What a long, low house means for the lights

A one-story ranch stretched across a wide lot gives a long eave at a modest height. We charge for a roofline by its length in feet, so on a house like that the length matters more to the total than the height does. Tell us if you only want the side facing the street.

A big lot can also put the outlet far from where the lights hang. Power a long way off can nudge a quote, so mention where your outdoor or garage outlets are when you send photos.

Any tree you want wrapped gets its own line on the quote, priced from a picture taken far enough back to fit it all in.

Few associations, one gated exception

Villa Park has no citywide association, and most homes belong to none. Villa Park Orchards, a small gated community built in 1993, is one of the few with an HOA and dues. If you live there, read what it says about holiday decor and type its name into the form. Working within HOA rules.

Long frontages, far outlets and trees

Our own questions, based on how Villa Park homes are laid out. Nobody from the city asked them.

Can I light only part of a long front?

Yes. The quote covers the edges you choose, measured in feet, so leaving the far wing dark simply lowers the footage. Point out the stretch you want when you send photos.

The outlet is nowhere near the roofline. Can you still do it?

Yes, just say so up front. We need one working outlet and check it on install day, and our cords run along the eave or behind a downspout where they are least seen. A long run to power can shift the quote within its range. Cords, outlets and timers.

Should the hedges or trees be part of it?

Only if you want them. Hedges or a wrapped tree can be added one at a time without moving up to the whole-front package. Hedges or roofline only and wrapping trees.

175 feet of ranch eave, worked through

Long frontages call for a long example, so take 175 feet. It is chosen for the arithmetic only and is not a measured Villa Park home. At $9.50 per foot on our lights, 175 feet comes to $1,662.50. The bulbs, timer, clips and labor are all inside that figure, along with any repair before January and the takedown itself. Trees, hedges and wreaths are added separately, each from photos. Lights you supply have their own rate on the pricing page.
