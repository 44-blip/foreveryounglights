Christmas tree delivery

Can I Have a Real Christmas Tree in an Apartment, Office or Shop in California?

California's fire code allows a cut Christmas tree inside your own apartment or condo unit, but your lease, your building's rules and your city can be stricter. For a shared room or a workplace, ask before you order.

  • One-family or two-family house: not on the list of building types in section 806.1.1.
  • Inside your own apartment or condo unit: the code allows it. Your lease or the building's rules can still say no.
  • Shared room or workplace: ask first. The answer depends on how the room is used, and it can call for approved sprinklers, flame-retardant treatment or a treatment tag.
  • Care facilities: some prohibit cut trees even with sprinklers. Ask the facility and the local fire department.
  • Never in an exit, corridor, lobby or vestibule, and never blocking the way out.

This is a summary of state rules, not a ruling on your building. How a room is used, city amendments, leases and HOA or building rules can all change the answer. Confirm the exact room with the building manager and the local fire department before you order. This page is information, not legal advice.

Inside your home

Section 806.1.1 makes an exception for individual dwelling units in apartment and condo buildings, called Group R-2: the code allows a cut tree inside the unit. The exception covers the unit itself, not hallways, lobbies or shared rooms. It does not override your lease or the building's own rules, so check those before you order.

One-family and two-family houses are Group R-3, which section 806.1.1 does not list.

Shared rooms and workplaces: ask first

For a shared room or workplace, ask the manager how the room is used. Before you order, ask the local fire department whether a cut tree is permitted there and whether that spot requires approved sprinklers, flame-retardant treatment or a treatment tag. Some care facilities prohibit cut trees even with sprinklers.

Two state rules are behind those questions. Section 806.1.1 limits cut trees in many shared and public uses, in some of them to areas with an approved automatic sprinkler system. Title 19, section 3.08 can also require the tree to be treated and kept flame retardant. Which of them applies depends on how the room is classed, and one building can hold more than one use.

Routes out, candles and care buildings

Section 806.2 keeps cut trees out of exits, corridors, lobbies and vestibules, and the way out must stay clear. An approved sprinkler system does not lift this location rule. Section 806.3 bars candles and open flames on or near the tree.

Care settings do not all follow one rule. Some prohibit cut trees even with sprinklers. For a hospital, clinic, care home, day care or similar setting, ask the facility and the local fire department before arranging a tree.

Source: 2025 California Fire Code, chapter 8, section 806. Title 19, section 3.08 is printed in the same chapter.
