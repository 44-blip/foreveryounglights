Guide

C9 bulbs, mini lights, or permanent LEDs?

Three very different looks that people often talk about as if they were the same thing. They are not, and picking the wrong one is the most common regret we hear.

What is a C9?

A C9 is the big, round, strawberry-shaped bulb most people picture when they picture Christmas lights. It is the classic. Spaced evenly along a roofline, it reads as a clean line of individual points from the street, and it still looks like a bulb up close rather than a strip of light.

Modern C9s are LED, so they are cool to the touch, they use very little power, and a long run does not trip anything. The shape is traditional; the technology behind it is not.

This is what we install on both packages. It is the right answer for most houses because it is the look that reads as Christmas from a hundred feet away, at night, from a moving car — which is the actual viewing condition for a roofline display.

What about mini lights?

Mini lights are the small ones, closely spaced, the kind most people already own for a tree. They are excellent at some jobs and poor at others.

Where they shine is wrapping. Wrapping a trunk, a branch, a column or a railing needs a light small enough to sit tight against a curved surface, and a C9 is too big and too far apart to do that well. Every wrapped tree and wrapped column we do uses a small light for exactly this reason.

Where they struggle is the roofline. From the street, closely spaced minis blur into a thin glowing line rather than reading as distinct bulbs. It can look good on a modern house, but it lacks the punch of a C9 and it looks noticeably less "Christmas."

In practice, most good displays use both: C9 on the roof, small lights on the wraps. That is what the Premium Full Front package actually is.

What are permanent roofline LEDs?

A permanent system is a track of tiny color-changing LED points installed under the eave line and left there year round. You control it from your phone. Each point can be any color, so the same run does warm white in June, orange in October and red and green in December.

It is a genuinely different product. It is a fixture on the house rather than a seasonal service, it goes in once, and nothing comes down in January.

The trade-off is the look. A permanent track gives you clean, even, modern lines — closer to architectural lighting than to Christmas lights. It cannot reproduce the fat individual bulb of a C9, and if that classic look is the thing you want, no amount of color control replaces it.

Which one suits my house?

A few honest rules of thumb:

  • You want it to look like Christmas. C9 on the roofline. Nothing else comes close.
  • You want trees, columns and railings lit. Small lights, wrapped. Combine with C9 above.
  • You want the house lit for more than one holiday. Permanent. That is the whole point of it.
  • You have a very modern house with clean lines. Permanent suits the architecture better than a bulb line does.
  • You might move in a few years. Seasonal. You are not paying for a fixture you leave behind.
  • Your HOA is strict about permanent exterior changes. Seasonal, and check the rules first.

Can I have both?

Yes, and some people do. A permanent track handles the roofline all year, and we still come out in November to wrap the trees, light the entry and run the pathway. You get the year-round flexibility on the roof and the real depth of a full display for the six weeks that matter most.

What about warm white versus multicolor?

This is a separate choice from bulb type and it matters just as much. Warm white reads as elegant and architectural, it flatters older and Spanish-style homes, and it never clashes with anything. Multicolor reads as festive and playful, it is what most kids picture, and it carries further at night.

Neither is more expensive. If you are torn, look at your own front door: whatever is already there in brick, tile or stucco usually tells you which one will sit better against the house.
