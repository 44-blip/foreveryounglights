HOA rules and Christmas lights

A lot of Orange County lives under an HOA. Here is what boards usually care about, and how to get a display approved without a fight.

Will my HOA actually stop me?

Almost never. Holiday lighting is one of the few exterior changes most communities actively expect and welcome, and plenty of boards run their own decorating contests.

What boards do have are rules about the details — usually dates, colors, and how the display is attached. Those rules exist because a handful of displays every year go up in October, stay up until March, or use something the community decided years ago it did not want. None of that is aimed at a normal roofline display.

So the honest answer is: your HOA will very likely say yes. The work is in reading the rules once, before the lights go up, rather than after a letter arrives.

What do HOA rules usually cover?

Rules vary a lot between communities, and we will not pretend to know yours without reading it. But most sets of guidelines touch the same handful of subjects:

  • Dates. The most common rule of all. Typically an earliest date lights may go up and a deadline for taking them down. This is the rule people get letters about.
  • Colors. Some communities allow anything. Some restrict to warm white or a defined palette, particularly where architecture is tightly controlled.
  • Attachment. Rules against drilling, nailing or permanently fixing anything to the exterior, roof or common-area walls.
  • Moving or flashing lights. Frequently restricted, especially on streets facing a main road.
  • Sound and inflatables. Often covered separately from lighting, and often stricter.
  • Where the display may go. Front elevation and private yard, usually yes. Common areas, shared walls and slopes, usually not without approval.

Do I need approval before you install?

That depends entirely on your community, and only your governing documents can answer it. Many treat seasonal lighting as pre-approved and need nothing from you. Some ask for a short architectural request, particularly for anything permanent.

The practical version: if you are having a standard seasonal display installed and taken down inside the usual dates, you very probably need nothing. If you are considering permanent roofline lighting, assume you need approval and start that conversation early, because a permanent fixture is a different category to most boards and approval can take weeks.

How do I find my rules?

Three places, in order of how fast they are. Your community's website or owner portal, where the current guidelines are usually posted. The CC&Rs and architectural guidelines you received when you bought. And the management company, who will normally answer a one-line email about holiday lighting the same week.

Ask one specific question rather than a general one. "What are the dates holiday lights may be up, and are there color restrictions?" gets a useful answer far faster than "what are the rules on Christmas lights?"

How do you design around HOA rules?

Tell us the name of your HOA when you ask for a quote — there is a field for it on the form — and anything you already know about the rules. We design inside them from the start, which is much easier than redoing a display after a complaint.

Two of the most common restrictions are easy for us to work with. If your community restricts colors, warm-white C9 is almost always acceptable and it looks excellent on the Spanish and traditional architecture that is common here. If your community bans anything drilled or nailed into the exterior, we already meet that rule on every job, because we use clips and never fasteners.

Date restrictions are simply a scheduling question. Tell us your earliest permitted date and your takedown deadline, and we will book inside both.

Can you talk to my board for me?

We cannot file an approval on your behalf, because boards deal with the homeowner, not the contractor. What we can do is give you everything a board normally wants to see: what is being installed, where it goes on the house, how it is attached, and the dates it will be up and down. That is usually enough to attach to a request.

If your board asks a technical question we have not covered, text us and we will answer it in writing so you can forward it.

What if I get a letter anyway?

Send us a photo of it. If the issue is something about the install — attachment, placement, a light spilling somewhere it should not — we will come out and fix it. If it is a dates question, we can usually move your takedown up.

We have no interest in a customer being in a dispute with their own community over work we did.

What this means for your quote

Name your HOA on the quote form and tell us any rule you already know about. It does not change the price. It changes the design, and knowing it up front is the difference between one install and two.
